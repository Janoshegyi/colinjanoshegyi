About

01

A few places, a few perspectives

Indonesia is where I was born. Cambodia is where I spent much of my life growing up, and where my family eventually settled. Hungary was where I went to university, spent a few years figuring life out, and discovered that studying business could lead me into rather more technical corners of the internet than I expected.

Living in different countries has made it difficult to look at things from just one perspective. Something that feels perfectly ordinary in one place can be completely unfamiliar somewhere else. Language, culture, and the little assumptions people make about the world all have a way of revealing themselves when you move somewhere new.

I've ended up learning seven languages to varying degrees of success. English, Indonesian, and Hokkien are the ones I grew up with. Mandarin, Dutch, Khmer, and Hungarian came later, with varying levels of fluency and varying amounts of suffering. (Especially Hungarian)

02

Work, more or less

Most of my professional life revolves around understanding how people find things on the internet, why they choose what they choose, and what happens between those two moments.

Funny enough, I once thought I'd end up working in HR. I considered myself reasonably good with people. Then I watched The Office, and Toby didn't exactly help the case.

Marketing eventually caught my interest. I started with social media, then moved further into SEO, where I found myself paying attention not just to what people see, but to how websites actually work. At Oska Digital Advertising, I contributed to content strategy as part of a team working to improve a resort company's organic search performance. Over six months, organic clicks from Google Search increased by 60% compared with the preceding six-month period, when I was still in training.

I enjoy this work, but I've become increasingly curious about what happens underneath the surface. A website can look perfectly fine to a visitor while having technical problems that make it harder for search engines to understand or index. Sometimes it's a missing piece of metadata. Sometimes the problem runs deeper, into how a page is built or delivered.

And sometimes, a website serves entirely different content depending on who's looking at it.

That last problem caught my attention enough that I decided to build something around it.

Personal Project

Cloaker Checker

Cloaker Checker is a tool I built to compare what a website serves to an ordinary visitor with what it serves to Googlebot. It helps investigate potential cloaking and compromised pages, including cases where a website appears harmless to a person but presents something very different to a search engine.

The idea sits somewhere between SEO, development, and investigation. You start with a discrepancy, ask why it exists, and try to establish what's actually happening. I like problems that require a little digging, especially when the obvious explanation isn't necessarily the right one.

Building the tool has also given me a reason to explore the technical side of SEO beyond the usual discussions about keywords, rankings, and content. Those things matter, of course. But understanding how a website behaves is just as interesting to me as understanding how people discover it.

You can read more about the project, try the tool, or browse some of the other things I've been building on the projects section of the homepage.

03

Things I've learned along the way

My years at the University of Szeged involved their fair share of academic work, cappuccino mornings, and espresso martini evenings. I received the MNB Excellence Scholarship from the Hungarian National Bank and the GTK Faculty Scholarship twice, and in 2025, I won first place in Prezilimpia, the university's public speaking competition in collaboration with Ezabeszéd Toastmasters Rhetoric Club, with a speech about AI as both a guardian and an invisible threat.

The speech began with a slice of burnt toast. There was a reason for that, although explaining it here would spoil the story. I've written about the experience separately.

I mention these things because they've been part of my life, not because I think a list of achievements tells you very much about a person. The more interesting part is usually what happened around them: the work that went into them, the things that didn't go to plan, and the occasional absurdity of finding yourself starting a speech about artificial intelligence by ranting about burnt toasts.

My academic background is in business administration and management, and my thesis explored marketing strategies for Indonesian food products in the global market. Looking back, there's a fairly clear thread connecting that work to what I do now. I'm interested in how people make decisions, how businesses reach them, and how the systems between the two actually work.

The subject has changed a little. The questions haven't changed as much as I might have expected.

04

Outside work

I also write. Or at least convince myself that I am writing something significant.

The things I write about don't always have much to do with SEO. Some are about ambition, some about disappointment, and others about the strange ways people try to make sense of their lives. Being in my twenties seems to provide a steady supply of material, and realizing that what we are going through is no stranger for others as well.

Every now and then, I write about something on the internet that has caught my attention. Other times, I start with an ordinary observation and end up somewhere I didn't expect.

I've always found it interesting how seriously we take certain things, particularly when we're young and convinced that life is a problem we can solve if we just make the right decisions. We make plans, compare ourselves with other people, and spend an unreasonable amount of time worrying about whether we're falling behind. Then something small happens, and we're reminded that the world doesn't necessarily care about any of it.

I'm not sure I've arrived at any particularly useful conclusions about this. It does, however, give me things to write about. If I can leave you with a thought, here's a sprinkle of wisdom from my professor, shared over dinner at a Chinese restaurant: "80% of our problems will solve itself. 20% of our problems gets worse by time. We need to focus on the 20 instead of the 80."

You'll find these pieces in Notes & Thoughts, alongside more practical writing about SEO, technology, and the projects I'm working on. Some are more serious than others. I make no promises about which category a particular article will fall into.

05

This website

I built this website as a place to keep my work and writing together. It's part portfolio, part notebook, and part record of things I've made or become interested in along the way.

Some of it is professional. Some of it is personal. Occasionally, the two overlap. I like to keep it that way. It feels more honest than pretending the person who works on websites and the person who writes about burnt toast are entirely separate individuals.

You'll find my professional work, experiments, and personal writing here. You can explore my projects, read my writing, or find me on LinkedIn and GitHub. If something catches your attention, or if you simply want to talk about something I've written, you're welcome to get in touch.

For now, I'm working on Cloaker Checker, continuing to explore the technical side of SEO, and writing whenever I have something worth writing about. The details will probably change. That's partly the point of having a personal website.

Thanks for stopping by and reading.
