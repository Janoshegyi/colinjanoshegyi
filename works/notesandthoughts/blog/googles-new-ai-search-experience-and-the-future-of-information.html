Google’s New AI Search Experience and the Future of Information

Google’s new AI Search is a real step forward in convenience and access. Its effect on how people find and trust information is less settled.

At Google I/O 2026 in May, Google announced the biggest upgrade to the Search box in over 25 years. Gemini 3.5 Flash is now the default model in AI Mode worldwide, the box accepts text, images, files, videos and Chrome tabs, and Google introduced background information agents and interfaces that build custom tools from a prompt.

Key takeaways

  • AI Mode passed one billion monthly users a year after launch, and Gemini 3.5 Flash is now its default model worldwide.
  • In Pew Research data, people clicked a result link in 8% of visits with an AI summary and 15% without, and clicked a link inside the summary in just 1%.
  • Chartbeat measured Google search referrals to news sites down 33% globally over twelve months, and the EBU and BBC found 45% of AI answers to news questions had a significant issue.

The scale makes this more than a product update. Google Search is one of the most widely used information systems ever built. When people read an AI summary instead of the sources behind it, questions about transparency, trust and responsibility for errors start to matter more than the convenience.

Much of the new technology is also useful. It could make research easier for people who have never been comfortable with search, and that deserves to be taken seriously.

What Google Actually Announced

The new Search box expands to fit longer, more conversational questions, and users can ask a follow-up on an AI Overview and continue into AI Mode. Both were live at announcement. The larger features came with conditions. Google said information agents and the mini-app builder would reach AI Pro and Ultra subscribers first this summer, while generative UI would be free for everyone.

The agents are the most significant change. They run in the background and send updates, so someone searching for an apartment, a flight or a product recall notice could hand the repetitive checking to Search. The multimodal box also reflects how people search now, since few of us rely on keywords alone.

The new search field expands to accommodate longer queries that are more conversational, aligning with the way one might type or talk into Gemini or ChatGPT.
- Lisa Eadicicco, CNN Business Tech

The longer questions matter for a measurable reason. AI summaries show up far more often on long, conversational searches, which is what the redesigned box invites, and what people do after seeing one is different.

The Benefits Are Real

Users without technical skills may gain the most. Tasks that once required specialist knowledge or hours of research could become much easier, and that matters even though the larger concerns are serious. Students can request an interactive simulation on the spot. Shoppers can evaluate a product from photos and reviews together. People without a personal assistant or paid services can hand off tasks that used to take up their time.

The reach matters as well. Personal Intelligence is rolling out to nearly 200 countries and territories in 98 languages, with no subscription required. Advanced digital tools have mostly arrived first in wealthier, English-speaking markets, so this could close part of that gap.

The Information Problem

Traditional search gave users links. It was imperfect and shaped by SEO, advertising and ranking incentives, but users still reached the original source. AI synthesis changes that relationship. Instead of comparing sources, users get a single confident summary.

Writers describe a related trap as the Tiffany Problem. Tiffany sounds like a modern name, so readers assume it would be out of place in a medieval story. It is not. The name goes back centuries, from the Greek Theophania. The facts were sound, but readers trusted what sounded believable over what was true.

Language models pull in the same direction. They produce fluent, natural answers, and a confident answer that sounds right can easily outperform a more careful one that is actually accurate. Whether an answer is correct matters, and so does whether users can see how it was reached.

Misinformation amplification

Information agents draw on blogs, videos, social discussions and live web content. That is convenient, but an inaccurate claim can spread through AI summaries before anyone has verified it.

Reduced source transparency

The smoother and more conversational an answer becomes, the less likely users are to question it. Many will take the conclusion without opening the sources behind it.

Publisher dependency

AI systems rely on publishers, journalists, forums, researchers and creators. If AI answers send less traffic to those sites, the revenue that pays for good information could shrink over time.

The Important Counterargument

Search was already flawed. SEO spam, content farms, manipulative ranking and clickbait all predate generative AI, and most users rarely checked sources even then. AI synthesis might improve information quality for the average user by cutting noise and filtering obvious misinformation better than ranking did. The question is therefore less whether AI belongs in Search than whether transparency and accountability will keep pace with it.

Google disputes the strongest readings of this data. Its head of Search has argued that third-party studies overstate declines in overall traffic, and the company says the clicks that remain are higher quality. Both sides can be partly right. Pew followed 900 people and DCN surveyed 19 publishers, and neither can see Google's own totals.

We already live in a world manipulated by AI, mostly recommendation algorithms at this point. They decide what we see. And in many ways that contributes significantly to how we understand our world.
- Hank Green

Where This Leaves the Open Web

Search has historically sent users to publishers, forums, researchers and independent sites. AI synthesis tends to keep them on Google. If users stop visiting sources, publishers lose advertising revenue and subscriptions, and the systems that depend on the open web end up weakening what sustains it.

Smaller publishers, independent sites and original analysis are the most exposed. The relationship between AI platforms and content creators will probably be one of the main economic questions of the next decade.

What This Means for SEO

Clicks are no longer the only measure of search visibility. With only 1% of visits clicking a link inside an AI summary, a page can be cited and still earn almost no traffic, so track impressions, brand mentions and branded searches next to sessions.

Original data gives AI summaries something worth citing and gives readers a reason to click through. Summaries repackage what already exists, so first-hand research, clear methods and named sources are harder to replace than a general explainer.

Question-style searches are where summaries appear most, so write headings that match real questions and answer each one directly in the first sentences. Pages that are easy to crawl and easy to quote are also the easiest for answer engines to use. Audiences built outside Google matter too, such as direct visitors, newsletters and video, which is where the Reuters Institute found publishers putting more effort.

Final Thoughts

This is a genuine technological milestone, and the gains in accessibility and convenience are significant. At this scale, though, Google shapes how people understand information: what they trust, which publishers survive, and how the public forms its picture of events.

Search will almost certainly become more AI-driven. It remains to be seen whether transparency, accountability and visible sources will keep pace with the influence these systems already have.
